I’m Xiangyu Zhang (you can call me Jack), a third-year PhD student in the [Liu Group](https://www.pengliugroup.com/) in the Department of Chemistry at the University of Pittsburgh, advised by Prof. Peng Liu. My research sits at the interface of computational chemistry and machine learning, combining quantum chemistry (DFT), classical and ab initio molecular dynamics, energy decomposition analysis, machine learning force fields, and hybrid QM/MM and ML/MM methods. I apply these tools across a broad range of chemical problems, from traditional transition-metal catalysis and radical chemistry to emerging areas such as single-atom catalysis and enzymatic catalysis. In parallel, I work on AI-for-chemistry problems and contribute to improving agent-accelerated computational workflows. Across these efforts, I strive to push the boundaries of computational chemistry and to maximize its contribution to the advancement of chemical science. I have co-authored seven papers in J. Am. Chem. Soc. (four), Angew. Chem. Int. Ed. (two), and Nat. Catal., including co-first-author work in J. Am. Chem. Soc. (two) and Angew. Chem. Int. Ed., with a further co-first-author manuscript under review at Science. I am also a recipient of the Ashe Merit Scholarship.

I received my B.S. in Chemistry from the College of Chemistry and Molecular Sciences at Wuhan University, where I worked with Prof. Xiaotian Qi. During my undergraduate years, I was an AI-for-Science intern at the Shanghai AI Laboratory (上海人工智能实验室) for two years, where I helped build several molecular and chemical-reaction databases. At Pitt, I have also served as a Teaching Assistant for the undergraduate Organic Chemistry Laboratory course for two years.

I am interested in interdisciplinary work at the boundary of AI/ML and chemistry, especially in industry.
